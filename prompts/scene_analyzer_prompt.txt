job is NOT to summarize the sentence — it is to describe exactly what
stock footage/photo would visually match it, so a media-search engine
can find the right clip and reject the wrong ones.

Video topic: {topic}

Think like a director briefing a stock-footage researcher. Many
narration sentences are abstract, scientific, or metaphorical (e.g.
"Your body would stretch into spaghetti" is about a black hole's tidal
forces, NOT about food or a kitchen). Infer the real visual scene, not
the literal words.

This also applies when the narration names something that is itself
real-world, but only as a REFERENCE inside a different subject. E.g. a
scene about video games that says "...like scoring a goal in a football
game" is about a PERSON PLAYING A VIDEO GAME (controller, screen,
console/PC) -- it must NOT return real-world football/soccer match
footage just because the word "football" appears. Always ask: what is
this scene actually ABOUT (the subject of the video/story), versus what
words does it merely MENTION in passing? "objects", "keywords_primary",
and "keywords_negative" must reflect the actual subject, and the literal
mentioned-but-not-the-subject term (e.g. "football", "soccer", "stadium")
should usually go in "forbidden"/"keywords_negative" instead of
"keywords_primary" when it would visually mislead a stock search.

Return ONLY JSON, no markdown fences, in exactly this shape:
{{
  "scene_type": "<one of: Scientific Simulation, Real World Footage, Historical Footage, Abstract/Conceptual, Interview/Talking Head, News/Documentary, Nature, Animation>",

  "objects": ["<2-6 concrete objects/subjects that MUST plausibly appear>"],
  "forbidden": ["<4-10 concrete objects/settings that would be WRONG for this scene and must NOT appear, e.g. unrelated everyday settings like 'Office', 'Podcast', 'Kitchen', 'Cars', 'People Talking' when the scene is about space/science>"],

Rules:
- Domain Lock: unless this scene's own narration clearly moves to a

- "forbidden" must always be non-empty — even a plausible scene has things that would clearly be wrong (e.g. for a nature scene, forbid "office", "city street", "studio").
- Keep every keyword short (1-3 words), lowercase, stock-footage-search style.
- Do not explain your reasoning, return JSON only.
